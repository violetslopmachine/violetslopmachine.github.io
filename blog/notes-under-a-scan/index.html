Notes Under a Scan

2026-09-12

Dashes, abbreviations and a table of sizes, and what it takes to type them.

The captions under the portfolio pieces have been fighting me all week. A measurement wants a multiplication sign rather than the letter x. A range of years wants an en dash rather than a hyphen. And half the words I reach for want explaining to anyone who has not stood in a paper shop.

None of that is a typography problem. It is a notation problem, and the notation has to survive being typed into a plain text file on a Tuesday.

Three characters I keep needing

  • &times;, so that 760 &times; 1090 sets as 760 × 1090 and not as something you are meant to work out
  • &ndash;, so that 1998&ndash;2003 closes up the way a range should: 1998–2003
  • &frac12;, for the sheet that came back 89½ centimetres and would not be talked out of it

Each is a name rather than a keystroke, which means I can type it and still read the file a year later. The alternative is pasting a character I cannot see, cannot search for, and cannot tell from the one next to it.

The one that needs markup instead

Paper weight is given in gsm everywhere on this site, and an abbreviation is not a character — it is a word with something attached to it. That wants a tag, and a tag written in the middle of a sentence stays a tag now.

A sheet at 300 gsm is roughly what most people mean by card. Below 200 it will cockle the moment anything wet touches it, which I learned twice.

The sizes themselves go in a block, indented with a tab so the columns hold:

Long Horizon	760 x 1090
Wide Water	420 x 594
Slow Return	297 x 420

Nothing in there is markdown and nothing in there should be. A code block is the one place a column of numbers is allowed to be a column of numbers, and the tabs inside it are left exactly as they were typed — which is the whole reason the letter x is back.
