On Colour

2026-08-20

Notes on choosing a colour and then living with it for a week.

A colour looks different depending on what sits next to it, which is obvious until you try to choose one on its own and find that you cannot.

Picking one

The green above is #3F6B54. Alone it reads as forest. Beside anything warm it turns grey and sulks. I kept it for a week to find out whether I would tire of it, and I did, on the fourth day.

Three others went up on the wall before the green did:

  • A blue that turned grey every afternoon at four
  • A red I liked for about an hour
  • A yellow that made everything beside it look dirty

A colour is a relation and not a thing, which is why a chart of them tells you almost nothing.

Written down, the week came out like this:

mon  #3F6B54
tue  #3F6B54
wed  #3F6B54
thu  #C08A3E

The ochre replaced it and has the opposite problem. It is friendly to everything, which after a while reads as having no opinion at all.
